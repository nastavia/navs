# NAVS-421: Voluntary Standard for Regulation-Aligned Artificial Intelligence (RAAI)
## Reference Framework

**Developed by Nastavia** (www.nastavia.com)

---

## 1. Purpose

The **NAVS-421** standard establishes a common reference framework for designing Artificial Intelligence systems that continuously maintain alignment with regulatory, organizational, and operational requirements through controlled coevolution.

Rather than prescribing specific algorithms, AI models, or implementation technologies, the standard defines the minimum architectural components and governance principles required to support trustworthy adaptation, regulatory alignment, traceability, verification, and validation throughout the AI system lifecycle.

---

## 2. Scope

NAVS-421 applies to AI systems operating in regulated or safety-critical environments, including, but not limited to:

- Financial services
- Public administration
- Healthcare
- Critical infrastructure
- Industrial automation
- Defence and security
- Autonomous systems
- Any domain requiring demonstrable governance and regulatory compliance

The standard is technology-neutral and may be implemented using machine learning, generative AI, symbolic AI, hybrid AI, agentic AI, or future AI paradigms.

---

## 3. Design Principles

NAVS-421 is founded on the following principles:

1. **Regulatory alignment** shall be treated as an architectural property rather than a post-deployment compliance activity.

2. **Governance and knowledge** shall evolve independently while remaining continuously synchronized through controlled feedback.

3. AI systems shall generate sufficient evidence to support **verification, validation, auditability, and accountability**.

4. AI systems shall remain capable of learning and adaptation while operating within explicitly defined governance boundaries.

5. The reference framework shall remain independent of implementation technologies to encourage interoperability and future evolution.

---

## 4. Reference Framework

NAVS-421 consists of two complementary components:

- a four-layer reference architecture defining the structural organization of a Regulation-Aligned AI system;
- a controlled coevolutionary feedback mechanism governing the interaction between AI systems and their regulatory environment.

Both components are considered essential parts of the framework.

---

## 5. Four-Layer Reference Architecture

The NAVS-421 architecture consists of four logical layers.

| Identifier | Layer | Purpose |
|------------|-------|---------|
| **RL-1** | Hardware Layer | Provides the trusted execution environment and establishes the operational foundation of the AI system. |
| **RL-2** | Immutable Layer | Contains permanent axiomatic constraints and fundamental rules that cannot be modified during normal operation. |
| **RL-3** | Mutable Layer | Maintains regulations, organizational policies, and operational rules that evolve through controlled governance processes. |
| **RL-4** | Knowledge Layer | Contains the AI model and its evolving knowledge while remaining constrained by the underlying regulatory layers. |

The standard intentionally does not prescribe implementation technologies or internal designs for any individual layer.

---

## 6. Controlled Coevolution

Unlike conventional AI architectures, NAVS-421 treats regulatory alignment as a continuous process rather than a static configuration.

The framework establishes a closed feedback mechanism in which:

- the operational environment continuously generates new observations;
- AI systems produce decisions and operational evidence;
- governance processes evaluate that evidence;
- regulations, organizational policies, and operational requirements evolve;
- updated constraints are incorporated into the regulatory layers;
- AI systems continue operating within the updated governance boundaries.

This continuous interaction allows both the AI system and its governing environment to evolve together while preserving safety, traceability, accountability, and regulatory alignment.

---

## 7. Layer Independence

Each architectural layer has a distinct responsibility and lifecycle.

The framework recommends minimizing direct dependencies between layers wherever practical. This allows regulatory evolution without unnecessary retraining of AI models while enabling AI capability improvements without compromising governance constraints.

---

## 8. Technology Neutrality

NAVS-421 intentionally does not prescribe:

- AI architectures;
- machine learning algorithms;
- large language models;
- knowledge representation techniques;
- rule languages;
- programming languages;
- hardware platforms;
- databases;
- communication protocols;
- deployment models.

These implementation decisions remain outside the scope of this standard.

---

## 9. Future Standard Family

NAVS-421 establishes the foundational reference framework for future RAAI standards, which may define, among others:

- rule representation;
- Regulation as Code (RaC);
- Policy as Code (PaC);
- domain-specific languages (DSL);
- verification methodologies;
- validation methodologies;
- evidence generation;
- traceability mechanisms;
- coevolution protocols;
- interoperability between RAAI implementations.

---

## 10. Compliance and Governance

NAVS-421 is a voluntary standard developed by **Nastavia** and offered for public use to encourage interoperable, transparent, and regulation-aligned AI systems.

An implementation may be considered aligned with NAVS-421 when it:

- implements the four-layer reference architecture;
- establishes a documented coevolutionary feedback mechanism;
- maintains clear separation between immutable constraints, mutable regulations, and AI knowledge;
- provides evidence supporting verification, validation, traceability, and auditability.

Conformance to NAVS-421 does not require any specific implementation technology, AI model, algorithm, programming language, or infrastructure, provided the architectural principles and governance objectives defined by this standard are preserved.
